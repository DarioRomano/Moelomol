# Art direction

- **Status:** Draft, for the lead's review. Nothing here is final until shown on
  screen in Milestone 2 renders.
- **Decided inputs:** Q1 → ADR-0008 (640×360, 16 px tiles); Q2 → placeholders
  in code now, final art provisioned later by the lead; Q9 → ADR-0010 (the pet).
- **Q13 decided:** people were taken town by town; monsters are wildlife and
  land changed by the god's presence (`story.md`).
- **Binding constraints:** ADR-0005 as amended by ADR-0010 (one base, peaceful
  farming, no human characters, the pet, environmental storytelling, lonely
  tone).

## What the art must make the player feel

1. **Alone, but not hopeless.** The world is empty of people, not empty of
   beauty. Quiet, not grim.
2. **The base is the one warm place.** Coming home should feel like relief.
3. **Someone was here.** Every area shows traces of the people who left:
   their tools, their unfinished work, their notes.
4. **Small in a large world.** The character is small on screen and the views
   are wide.

## Core rules

**Warmth means life.** Warm colours (amber, honey, firelight) are reserved for
the base, the player, growing crops and lights the player kindles. The wider
world is cool and desaturated. If a warm colour appears out in the world, it
means something: a note, an artefact, a sign that someone lived there. This
gives the player a reading rule without words.

**Distance from the base shows the calamity.** Areas further from the base are
more changed. The base is untouched because the pet enjoys it (ADR-0010). The
change is wildlife and land altered by the god's presence (Q13), so the
gradient runs from
overgrown-but-healthy near the base to strange and drained far out, using the
"changed land" colours more and more.

**Readability before detail.** Interactable things (crops, forage, notes,
chests, enemies) must read at a glance against the background, using value
contrast first and colour second. Backgrounds stay lower in contrast than
anything the player can touch.

**Traces, not bodies.** No corpses or gore. The absence of people is shown by
what they left behind: a set table, a fishing line left in the water, a child's
drawing pinned to a door, a field half-harvested.

**One pixel grid.** Every sprite, tile and UI element uses the same art pixel
size; nothing is scaled by non-whole amounts, rotated off-grid, or drawn at a
different pixel density. (Q1 decides the size of that grid.)

## The pet

- Outwardly an ordinary, lovable cat, drawn in the warm base palette like
  everything else that is alive and loved. It must be cute first.
- **Proposal:** a few deliberate "wrong" details that are easy to miss and
  never explained: its eyes catch the "changed land" violet at night; its
  shadow is sometimes a frame late or the wrong shape; it sits in places it
  could not have reached. These support the reveal in `story.md` and need the
  lead's approval.
- Size: about 12 × 10 px on the 16 px grid, small next to the 16 × 24 px
  player.
- Poses needed eventually: sit, walk, sleep, eat (treats), look at the player.

## Scale (ADR-0008)

With the 640×360 base and 16 px tiles: the player character about
16 × 24 px, crops 16 × 16 px per stage, notes and trinkets 8–16 px icons in the
world. A wide view (40 × 22 tiles) leaves room for emptiness around the
character.

## Light and time

A day–night cycle is not yet a decided system (it is not in the brief). If one
is added, dusk at the base is the emotional peak of the day: the warm lights
come on and the world beyond goes blue. This is noted here only as a direction,
not as a commitment to build the system.

## User interface

- Diegetic where cheap: notes look like paper the character found, not like
  game windows.
- UI uses the same pixel grid and palette as the world; pixel font only.
- Calm and sparse. No celebratory pop-ups; progress is shown quietly.

## Placeholder plan (Q2 decided: placeholders now)

First placeholder set, generated by script, palette-locked:
1. A ground tileset for the base: grass, tilled soil, path, water edge.
2. One crop in four growth stages.
3. A player character standing in four directions.
4. The pet sitting and walking.
5. One note prop and one warm light prop.
6. A showcase scene containing all of the above, rendered at several window
   sizes for review.

Every placeholder is listed in the asset register as placeholder.
