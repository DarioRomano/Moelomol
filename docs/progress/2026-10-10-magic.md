# 2026-10-10: Magic and the status-effect system

**Asked:** the other weapons, after Q20 = A; this is the last of three PRs
(hammer #19, bow #20, magic). Stacked on #20.

## Done

- **Status effects** (`src/combat/status_effects.gd`, one per fighter):
  - Smoulder, Chill and Rot: max 5 stacks; 6 s, refreshed by every
    application; then one stack falls off per second.
  - Damage over time once a second. Rot raises damage taken from everything
    and slows poise refill. Chill slows creature movement and attack timing.
  - At 5 stacks: Smoulder spreads, Chill freezes, Rot halves armour.
  - Creatures can resist an effect (rule only; no arena creature does yet).
  - Any `CombatMove` can carry an effect, so crafted arrowheads and
    enhancements need no new system.
- **Magic** (`src/combat/magic.gd`):
  - Ember bolt; Frost shard (heavy tap); Rot pool (heavy held 250 ms).
  - Release: consumes every stack into a burst, ×2 / ×3 for two or three
    effects, plus the combinations Shatter, Blight bloom and Brittle.
  - Focus, with the faster refill after 3 s unhit.
  - Casting slows but never roots; a hit in the windup interrupts.
  - Wardstep: a blink that leaves a Chill pool, invulnerable throughout.
  - A perfect hammer strike counts as a Release (the design's proposal).
  - Values and the readings taken of the design are in
    `docs/design/combat.md`, "Magic and status effects as built".
- **Changes to existing pieces:**
  - The dodge's invulnerable window is now a per-dodge field
    (`Fighter.iframes`); the roll is unchanged.
  - `Poise.tick` takes a refill scale (Rot).
  - Zones can add stacks without hitting (pools).
  - Spell projectiles have their own speeds.
  - F7 cycles magic too.
- **Drawing:**
  - The lantern; ember and frost bolts.
  - Rot and Chill pools, and the ring showing where a Rot pool will land.
  - Effect pips above creatures; the Frozen tint.
  - Release, Shatter, Blight bloom, Brittle and Wardstep effects.
  - A Focus bar in the HUD.
- **Rumble:** cast buzz, spell hit, Release (small and big by stacks
  consumed), Wardstep.
- **Renders:** `magic_poses.tscn` (ember on a smouldering creature, Frozen,
  Rot pool, three-effect Release, Brittle, Wardstep against a lunge).
- **Tests:** `tests/unit/test_combat_magic.gd` (28 tests). Suite: 197
  passing.
- **Playtest checklist:** `docs/playtests/2026-10-10-magic.md`.

## What broke and how it was found

- **I wiped `combat_sim.gd` while editing.** One line in my edit script
  opened the file for writing before reading it. The script printed
  nothing; a follow-up `grep` found the file empty. I restored it from the
  bow commit and reapplied the edits from a saved script. No committed work
  was lost. Lesson for my tooling: no in-place read-and-write in one
  expression.
- **A deliberate-break run timed out** and left one break in place
  (`_tick_effects` disabled). Before doing anything else I checked every
  break pattern against the files, restored the one left broken, confirmed
  the suite was green, and reran with a longer timeout.
- **Two of my tests were wrong** at first:
  - One expected 3 Rot stacks from a pool, but a pool gives a stack as it
    lands as well (4 is right).
  - One assertion message contained "60% speed", which the `%` operator
    rejects. The runner's logged-error hook caught it; it is now `%%`.
- **Rot pips vanished** into the grass in the first magic render (violet on
  dark green). Pips now sit on a dark strip and are slightly lightened;
  checked at 1920×1080 and 1280×720.
- **Deliberate breaks:** 25, one per rule (cap, refresh, Rot multiplier,
  Release formula, spread, Frozen, Chill slow in both places, armour
  weakening, resist, Shatter, Bloom, Brittle, hammer-as-Release, cast
  movement, interrupt, Focus need and refill, Wardstep iframes, pool and
  walls, pools not hitting, damage over time, clearing on respawn, the
  big-Release rumble). Every one makes a test fail.

## Not done (and why)

- Effect-resistant creatures in the arena: the roster is designed with the
  areas (the rule and its test exist).
- Creatures applying effects to the player: no creature design asks for it.
- DualSense trigger resistance on the pool hold: the later prototype.
- Feel, readability and real-machine performance: on the playtest
  checklist.
